# Batch layer and Batch Trend

Grouping plasma steps into batches, one sampled row per batch, and the tab that reads them.

## `BatchTracker` + `BatchCsvLogger` (+ `LeakMonitorSettings.Batch`)

**the cross-batch layer**: plasma steps grouped into batches, each batch reduced to one row taken at a fixed sampling point. It exists because the per-frame monitor answers "is it leaking now" against a baseline, while the question this chamber poses is "is this batch different from the last forty" — and that cannot be asked frame by frame, since the viewport fouls measurably within every batch and recovers when the chamber is cleaned (a process step's absolute brightness falls to 0.70 of the batch's first while a near-infrared / ultraviolet ratio moves by 1.8×). Two frames from different points in a batch are not comparable; the same point in two batches is. The sampling point is the **first complete step of a class**, median over **gate-open + 10–30 s** — first rather than best or averaged (later steps sit further into the fouling), and that window because a step's first 10 s scatter 8.7 % against 1.6 % over 10–30 s (the ignition transient) while the last frames are the recorder's stop-confirm tail, where the reference has collapsed and the ratio diverges (CV 136–241 %). Batch boundaries are a gap over `BatchGapSeconds` (240; measured gaps are 14–178 s inside a batch and 332 s+ between) **or** a step of `BatchStartClass` lasting at least `BatchStartMinDurationSeconds` — a threshold and a fact, and the duration is load-bearing because the same class also runs briefly *inside* a batch. Decided when the step ends, since the anchor rule needs its duration. **Deliberately uses neither the EMA nor the live σ** — a median is the smoothing — which is what makes it immune to the effect measured in `docs/leak-monitor-plan-zh-TW.md` §9.3, where a step change inflates the live σ enough to carry its own alarm threshold up with it; same separation, same reason, as `RatioCsvLogger` being independent of the intensity logger. A median rather than a mean because the gate discards isolated blank frames but a mean over ten points is still moved by one survivor near the floor. Writes **two files**: `{prefix}_Batch_*.csv` in the day folder, wide (one column per ratio), so a day folder copied off the machine still explains itself; and `_batches.csv` at the data root, **long** (one row per batch per ratio), because a trend spans dozens of day folders and a wide file's column set changes the moment a ratio is added, renamed or re-scoped — it is rebuildable from the day files, so it is a convenience, not a second source of truth, and the leading underscore keeps `Recording.TryParse` from listing it. A ratio whose class did not run in a batch is left blank rather than filled from another process's step. Two consequences in the engine: `AdvanceStep` now tolerates gate closures shorter than `MaxDropoutSeconds` (an isolated blank frame used to end the step, restarting classification mid-step and splitting one step into several downstream), and it tracks steps **even with no classifier configured** — a step is a fact about the tool, and re-deriving its boundaries in the batch layer would be a second definition of one.

## `BatchIndexReader` + `BatchTrendViewModel` + `BatchTrendPanel`

the **Batch Trend tab**, where the cross-batch layer above is actually read. One point per batch per ratio, from the long-format `_batches.csv` rather than the day files, because a trend spans dozens of day folders; columns are located by header name (so a column appended later is ignored, not fatal) and the file is opened with `FileShare.ReadWrite`, since the logger appends to it for as long as the tool runs. **The band is `1.4826 × MAD`, not a standard deviation** — the excursion it exists to catch does not go away on the next batch, and a standard deviation is widened by the very batches it is meant to flag: on the twelve batches `BatchIndexReaderTests` pins (nine normal, three 30 % high), the robust band puts them 40 σ out and a standard deviation puts them at 1.7 σ, i.e. invisible. Same trap as the live σ inside a step (`docs/leak-monitor-plan-zh-TW.md` §9.3), one level up — and the same reason the sampling point is a median. Normalized by default (`× own median`, said on the axis) because the three processes' ratios sit an order of magnitude apart; the ±3 σ band is drawn **once**, for the widest series, since three overlapping translucent bands say less than none. Each row states how many σ the newest batch sits from that ratio's own centre — the number the page exists for — while the rule that reads it (three consecutive batches past 3 σ) is printed on the page rather than coded, because until P3′ this page raises no alarm. A ratio with fewer than four batches gets no series at all, and with no index yet the axes are hidden rather than drawn from OxyPlot's defaults: an empty chart that looks populated is worse than a blank panel. The tab is deliberately ungated — reading a trend is an Operator concern, the same split as the recorder strip.
